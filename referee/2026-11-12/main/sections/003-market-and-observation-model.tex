\section{Market and Observation Model}
%% ============================================================

Two symmetric firms $i\in\{1,2\}$ simultaneously choose continuous prices $p_{i,t}\in[1,\,2.5]$. Marginal cost is $c=1$, product quality is $v=2$, outside option utility is $0$, market size is $1$, and the logit scale parameter is $\mu=0.25$. For firm $i$ against rival $j$, logit demand, stage profit, and the simulator's reported total surplus index are
\begin{align}
q_i(p_i,p_j) &= \frac{\exp\!\bigl((v-p_i)/\mu\bigr)}
{1+\exp\!\bigl((v-p_i)/\mu\bigr)+\exp\!\bigl((v-p_j)/\mu\bigr)},\\[3pt]
\pi_i(p_i,p_j) &= (p_i - c)\,q_i(p_i,p_j),\\[3pt]
\mathrm{TS}(p_i,p_j) &= (v-c)\bigl[q_i(p_i,p_j)+q_j(p_i,p_j)\bigr].
\end{align}
On a $1{,}501$-point reference grid, the symmetric one-shot Nash price is $p^{\mathrm{N}}\approx 1.472927$ and the symmetric joint-profit-maximizing price is $p^{\mathrm{J}}\approx 1.924981$. We measure normalized price elevation as
\[
E = \frac{\bar{p}-p^{\mathrm{N}}}{p^{\mathrm{J}}-p^{\mathrm{N}}},
\]
which records where average price $\bar{p}$ lies relative to the Nash ($E=0$) and joint-profit ($E=1$) benchmarks without clipping to $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Gaussian Monitoring and Channel Replacement}

At the start of each episode, a monitoring parameter vector $\theta=(\theta_{\mathrm{lo}},\theta_{\mathrm{hi}},\sigma_\varepsilon)\in\Theta$ is drawn uniformly from a retained MCMC calibration posterior and held fixed for the episode (full calibration details appear in Appendix~B). Current prices determine the latent monitoring mean via the minimum price:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:latent}
m_t = \theta_{\mathrm{lo}}
+ (\theta_{\mathrm{hi}}-\theta_{\mathrm{lo}})
\cdot \frac{\min(p_{1,t},p_{2,t})-p^{\mathrm{N}}}
{p^{\mathrm{J}}-p^{\mathrm{N}}},
\end{equation}
clamped to $[\theta_{\mathrm{lo}},\theta_{\mathrm{hi}}]$. Under the \textbf{informative channel}, the public report averages the two most recent latent means and adds a Gaussian innovation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:report}
r_{t+1} = \frac{m_t + m_{t-1}}{2} + \varepsilon_t,\qquad
\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
Reports pass through a one-period queue delay, so $r_{t+1}$ enters the observation window only after period-$(t+1)$ actions are chosen. Delivered reports are normalized by centering at $1.6$, dividing by $0.4$, and clipping to $[-5,5]$. Under the \textbf{action-independent ablation channel}, reports are drawn directly as $r_{t+1}\sim\mathcal{N}(1.6,\,0.4^2)$ independently of prices, keeping delay, observation dimensions, and market primitives unchanged. Because this replacement alters both action dependence and marginal report moments, comparing retrained policies identifies the effect of the full channel replacement rather than a pure information effect with matched moments (Appendix~B.2).
