\section{Experimental Evidence Across Training Seeds}
%% ============================================================

We train ten independent seed pairs from scratch under both the informative and action-independent channels. Each run logs 100 diagnostic checkpoints (16 episodes each) across training and conducts a final evaluation over 64 episodes (seed-by-seed outcomes are reported in Appendix~D).

\begin{figure}[t]
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{media/image1.png}
\caption{Deterministic deployment across training. Solid lines average ten training seeds; shaded bands show pointwise 95\% confidence intervals from 2{,}000 whole-seed bootstrap resamples. Zero elevation corresponds to the one-shot Nash price $p^{\mathrm{N}}$.}
\label{fig:elevation}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:elevation}, normalized price elevation starts near $0.43$ at the first checkpoint in both conditions ($0.4318$ informative vs.\ $0.4327$ action independent) as consequence of initial policy parameterization, and then declines steadily toward the Nash benchmark as learning progresses. By the final evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:final}), mean price elevation is $0.0151$ under informative monitoring and $0.0188$ under action-independent monitoring, with a paired difference of $0.0037$ (95\% bootstrap CI: $[-0.0241,\,0.0288]$).

Removing action information from the public channel therefore produces no statistically resolved reduction in converged prices or improvement in surplus. Because the confidence interval spans zero with ten seeds, this result shows the absence of a large channel effect rather than formal statistical equivalence. Effective feature rank (the exponential entropy of normalized eigenvalues of centered encoder features; Appendix~D) is slightly higher under ablation, reflecting the change in marginal report variance rather than a causal pricing mechanism.
