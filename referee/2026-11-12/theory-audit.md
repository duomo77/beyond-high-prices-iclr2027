# Exact claims and dependency checks

Revision reviewed: base `b119bdce823da664f1fdc577efa82241e1042631`, with the corrections in this branch. Mathematical derivations were checked by the coordinator and a separate researcher agent; implementation leaves remain conditional because code is absent.

| Claim card | Dependencies and checks | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Observation invariance: every post-intervention prescribed action and payoff agrees pathwise under frozen deterministic actors, coupled initial queues/innovations/time, action-independent reports, no additional action-dependent input and stationary stage payoffs | Equal reports and buffers imply equal actor inputs; induction gives equal subsequent actions; equal prices give equal stage payoffs. Persistent state and online updates are explicitly excluded. All 80 ablation mean paths are zero, corroborating the stored summaries only. | Proved under the stated assumptions; application to absent simulator code is conditional. |
| Specified-deviation threshold: $g,h>0$, $0\le\delta<1$, $0\le\alpha<\beta\le1$, bounded payoffs, absorbing credible $P$ | Subtracting Bellman equations gives $V^C-V^P=h/[1-\delta(1-\alpha)]>0$. Therefore $V^D-V^C=g-\delta(\beta-\alpha)h/[1-\delta(1-\alpha)]$. Positive denominator and $D=g(1-\alpha)+(\beta-\alpha)h>0$ justify division. Feasibility below one is equivalent to $(\beta-\alpha)h>\alpha g$. | Correct after the explicit $\beta>\alpha$ restriction. The original broader reading is refuted by the exact $27/19$ gain counterexample. |
| Exceptional specified deviations: $\beta\le\alpha$, $g,h>0$ | The same undivided expression gives $V^D-V^C\ge g>0$. | No admissible discount factor deters such a deviation. |
| All-action incentive inequality | For each firm and each action separately, the undivided Bellman comparison is $g(a)[1-\delta(1-\alpha)]\le\delta[\beta(a)-\alpha]h$. No division or positive-gain filter is applied to this universal statement. Credibility in $P$ is a separate hypothesis. | Correct as an incentive requirement; finite probes do not establish it over all actions/histories. |
| Gaussian comparative static: fixed actions/payoffs/means and $\mu_D<\tau<\mu_C$, $\sigma>0$ | $z_C<0<z_D$ gives $\alpha'=-z_C\phi(z_C)/\sigma>0$, $\beta'=-z_D\phi(z_D)/\sigma<0$. Differentiation of $g/D$ gives partials $g(g+h)/D^2$, $-gh/D^2$, $-g(\beta-\alpha)/D^2$. Chain rule gives the strictly positive noise derivative. The limits are $g/(g+h)$ and $2$. | Proved for the restricted fixed-trigger model. No implication for retrained finite-horizon PPO is asserted. |
| Finite tail: periods $0,\ldots,T-1$, $0\le H\le T-t_0-1$, each reward in $[0,B]$ | Each reward difference has magnitude at most $B$. Triangle inequality and the geometric series bound the omitted tail by $B\delta^{H+1}/(1-\delta)$. With $H=T-t_0-1$, the finite sum is empty. | Correct after the endpoint correction to $T-1$. |

The dependency graph has no mathematical cycle: model definitions → Bellman identities → specified-deviation IC → positive-denominator threshold/feasibility → Gaussian chain rule. The separate invariance branch is actor-input assumptions → input equality → action equality → payoff equality. Finite-horizon empirical probes do not serve as proof inputs for these infinite-horizon statements.

`scripts/verify_theory.py` checks the exact counterexample and corrected illustration, solves two static first-order conditions by bisection, and checks 1,000 sampled incentive cases plus eight noise settings. The floating-point noise check permits numerical ties where normal tails underflow; strict monotonicity follows from the analytic signs, not those numerical samples. External repeated-game papers provide context, not a hidden theorem needed by these elementary derivations.
